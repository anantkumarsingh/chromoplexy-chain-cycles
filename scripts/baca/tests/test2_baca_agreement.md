# Test 2: Formal Baca-vs-Ours Agreement

**Script:** `test2_baca_agreement.py`
**Output:** `results/stat_test2_baca_agreement.csv`
**Images:** `results/stat_test2_baca_agreement_contingency.png`, `results/stat_test2_baca_agreement_kappa.png`, `results/stat_test2_baca_agreement_dose_response.png`

## 1. What the test is

Phase 3 already computed, per patient, whether our real/obligate/probable
closure-based chromoplexy calls agree with Baca's own size-based criterion
(`*_agrees_with_baca_strict/loose` columns already exist in
`results/phase3_patient_chromoplexy_summary.csv`). What hasn't been done
yet is testing whether that agreement is statistically meaningful, or just
what you'd expect from two criteria that both tend to say "yes" most of the
time given how lopsided the population is (50 of 52 patients are
Baca-positive).

We chose **Fisher's exact test** over a chi-square test because several of
the 2x2 tables have a zero cell (e.g. zero patients who are Baca-negative
but ours-positive) — chi-square's approximation breaks down with expected
counts this small, Fisher's exact doesn't need that approximation.

We chose **Cohen's kappa** alongside the p-value because raw "percent
agreement" is misleading whenever one category dominates the marginal, as
it does here (Baca positive in 50/52 patients) — a criterion that just
always guesses "positive" would already agree with Baca 96% of the time by
accident. Kappa corrects for the agreement rate you'd expect from the
marginals alone by chance, isolating genuine concordance from marginal
imbalance.

We added a **Spearman dose-response check** as a third, complementary
angle: even where the binary agreement is weak, is there still a graded
relationship — do patients with more Baca-qualifying chains tend to achieve
a larger completed cycle span under our methods, even if they don't cross
the same binary threshold Baca does?

## 2. What the test does

**Part A** — for each of `real`/`obligate`/`probable` × `strict`/`loose`
(6 combinations), builds a 2x2 table of `baca_chain_has_chromoplexy` vs.
`<lens>_has_chromoplexy_<threshold>` across all 52 patients, runs Fisher's
exact test, and computes Cohen's kappa.

**Part B** — for each lens, takes the largest chromosome span any single
chain of a patient achieved (`<lens>_max_cycle_chrom_span`, maxed across
that patient's chains in the chain-level Phase 2 data) and correlates it
against `n_chains_baca_threshold` (how many of a patient's chains meet
Baca's ≥5-rearrangement criterion — an ordinal proxy for "how strongly
Baca-positive" that patient is) with Spearman's rho.

## 3. CSV column reference

| Column | Meaning | Source |

| `threshold` | `strict`/`loose` for Part A rows; blank for Part B (span is continuous, not threshold-gated) | `phase3_patient_chromoplexy_summary.csv` |
| `lens` | `real`, `obligate`, or `probable` | same as above |
| `n_baca_pos_ours_pos` | count of patients Baca calls positive AND our lens calls positive (Part A only) | derived |

| `n_baca_neg_ours_pos` | Baca negative, ours positive (Part A only) | derived |
| `n_baca_neg_ours_neg` | Baca negative, ours negative (Part A only) | derived |

| `n_patients` | number of patients included in that Part B correlation (Part B only) | derived |

## 4. Step-by-step: what the test does

1. Load `results/phase3_patient_chromoplexy_summary.csv` (52 patients) and `results/phase2_obligate_probable_completion.csv` (366 chains).
2. **Part A**: for each of 6 (lens, threshold) pairs, build the 2x2 table against `baca_chain_has_chromoplexy`, run `scipy.stats.fisher_exact`, and compute Cohen's kappa from the same 4 cell counts.
3. **Part B**: for each lens, group the chain-level CSV by `patient_id` and take the max of `<lens>_max_cycle_chrom_span`; merge that onto each patient's `n_chains_baca_threshold`; run `scipy.stats.spearmanr` on the pair.
4. Write all 9 rows (6 Part A + 3 Part B) to the output CSV.
5. Render three PNGs: a 2×3 grid of the 6 contingency tables as heatmaps, a bar chart of the 6 kappa values, and a 3-panel scatter of the dose-response relationship.

## 5. Limitations and Notes

- The 2-patient Baca-negative marginal severely limits statistical power for all 6 Fisher's exact tests. A non-significant p-value here means "underpowered to detect a relationship at this small n," not "no relationship exists."
- Kappa has a known instability with skewed marginals (sometimes called the "kappa paradox"): `obligate`/`loose` and `probable`/`loose` have 94% raw agreement (49/52) but kappa of only 0.371 ("fair" on the conventional Landis & Koch scale), because that raw rate is barely above what pure chance would produce given how dominant "Baca positive" already is in the marginal. Read kappa, not raw percent agreement, as the real agreement signal here.
- No multiple-testing correction is applied within this test file (6 Fisher's exact tests + 3 Spearman correlations = 9 tests). A combined Benjamini-Hochberg correction across this and the ETS+/− and ERG-heuristic tests is planned as a separate step later, not done per-test.
- The dose-response check uses the *maximum* achieved span across a patient's chains. For `obligate`/`probable`, `max_cycle_chrom_span` is null for any of a patient's chains that hit Phase 2's enumeration cap (12/366 chains cohort-wide) — if a patient's single largest chain happens to be one of those 12, their true best obligate/probable span could be understated here. Not corrected for.
- `odds_ratio = inf` occurs whenever the "Baca negative / ours positive" cell is 0 (true for 4 of the 6 tables) — this is an expected consequence of only having 2 Baca-negative patients in the cohort, not a computation error.

## 6. Analysis Corner

Step-by-step, what the results actually mean:

1. **None of the 6 Fisher's exact tests reach conventional significance** (all p > 0.1) — with only 2 Baca-negative patients in the whole cohort, there simply isn't enough data to formally confirm or rule out a relationship at the binary (positive/negative) level.
2. **Kappa never exceeds "fair" agreement** (max 0.371, for `obligate`/`loose` and `probable`/`loose`; as low as 0.007 for `real`/`strict`). Once you correct for how dominant "Baca positive" already is in this cohort, our closure-based criteria track Baca's size-based criterion only weakly at the binary level — this is consistent with, and adds statistical weight to, Phase 3's headline finding that only 4/50 of Baca's chromoplexy-positive patients have a REAL closed multi-chromosome cycle.
3. **A structural pattern worth flagging**: in 4 of the 6 tables (`real`/strict, `real`/loose, `obligate`/strict, `probable`/strict), zero patients are Baca-negative-but-ours-positive — our stricter/real-data criteria never call chromoplexy where Baca's size criterion doesn't already agree at least loosely. Only at the `loose` threshold under `obligate`/`probable` does a single patient break that pattern (1 patient qualifies under our loose completion criterion despite none of their chains reaching Baca's 5-rearrangement bar).
4. **The dose-response correlations tell a different, more encouraging story than the binary tables.** `real` shows no significant graded relationship (rho=0.221, p=0.115), but **`obligate` (rho=0.289, p=0.038) and `probable` (rho=0.351, p=0.011) both show a real, statistically significant positive relationship**: patients with more Baca-qualifying chains do tend to achieve a larger completed cycle span under invented completion — even though that relationship rarely crosses the same hard strict/loose threshold Baca's own criterion does.
5. **Bottom line:** Baca's chain-size criterion and our closure-based criteria are measuring related but clearly not interchangeable things. Binary agreement is weak and not formally significant given the small, skewed sample — but there is a real, significant continuous (dose-response) relationship for obligate/probable, not for real data alone. This nuance is worth stating carefully in the write-up: it's not that closure and chain-size are unrelated, it's that the binary threshold framing (which the strict/loose flags use) hides a real graded relationship that invented completion (but not real data by itself) is sensitive enough to pick up.
